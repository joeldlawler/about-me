# How I Lead

[← Back to Index](index.md)

## Leadership Philosophy

I aim to create clear thinking, steady systems, and teams that deliver meaningful work without burning out.

## Core Leadership Principles

### 1. Clear Thinking (Shane Parrish)

Slow down just enough to see second-order effects. I push explicit assumptions, pre-mortems, and simple decision rules so we avoid thrashing.

### 2. Mental Models (Shane Parrish)

Inversion, first principles, systems thinking for technical + organizational problems. Guides architecture decisions, migration planning, team investments.

### 3. Habits & Systems (James Clear)

Small, consistent improvements beat heroics. Lightweight rituals (PR hygiene, observability, prompt libraries) over big process overhauls.

### 4. Psychological Safety (Google's Project Aristotle)

Dependability, Structure & Clarity, Meaning, Impact—Google's 5 characteristics of effective teams are non-negotiable. I build environments where people can admit mistakes, ask questions, and connect work to mission-critical outcomes like healthcare compliance.

### 5. Culture & Belonging (Daniel Coyle)

Safety + high standards. People challenge ideas fearlessly while owning outcomes with accountability.

## Leadership Style

### Vision & Strategy

**Setting direction:**  
I start with clarity — identifying the most fundamental problems we're solving and why they matter. Using first-principles thinking and mental models, I define a strategic vision anchored in outcomes rather than outputs. Once the direction is clear, I co-create the roadmap with my leads to ensure alignment between the company's mission and the team's capabilities.

**Communicating vision:**  
I believe vision must be repeated, visualized, and personalized. I communicate it through storytelling, OKRs, and consistent touchpoints. My goal is to make every team member see how their work connects to the broader picture so that decisions at every level reinforce the same goals.

**Long-term thinking:**  
I balance short-term execution (ship fast, learn faster) with durable strategy. I think in systems — identifying leverage points, anticipating bottlenecks, and creating frameworks that scale beyond individuals. Strategic plans are not static documents; they evolve as we learn and grow.

### Team Development

**Mentoring:**  
I combine coaching and servant leadership. My first step is to understand each person's aspirations, strengths, and blind spots. I ask guiding questions and share mental models to help engineers think more critically, make sound judgments, and grow into independent problem-solvers.

**Growth opportunities:**  
I believe growth happens at the edge of comfort. I create stretch opportunities — leading initiatives, mentoring juniors, or contributing to cross-functional projects — with safety nets for learning. Career growth is a shared responsibility: I help identify paths, provide feedback, and clear roadblocks.

**Recognition:**  
Small wins build momentum. I recognize achievements publicly and specifically, connecting them to values like initiative, impact, or collaboration. I celebrate not just results but the behaviors that lead to long-term success.

**Feedback:**  
Following *Radical Candor*, I give feedback that is direct, timely, and caring. I aim for clarity, not comfort — always rooted in the person's growth and the team's success. I invite feedback on my leadership as well, modeling the culture I expect.

### Decision Making

**Process:**  
I rely on a "consider — decide — communicate" loop. I gather perspectives, challenge assumptions, weigh tradeoffs, and then decide with confidence. I prefer clarity over consensus when time is critical but transparency over speed when stakes are high.

**Delegation:**  
I delegate decisions to the level closest to the information — paired with clear intent and boundaries. This empowers teams while maintaining alignment. My role is to provide context, remove friction, and trust capable people to deliver.

**Consensus building:**  
I encourage constructive debate grounded in data, principles, and respect. The goal is not unanimous agreement but shared commitment. Once a decision is made, we move forward together.

**Accountability:**  
Accountability starts with ownership. I set clear expectations, measurable outcomes, and follow-up cadences. If we miss goals, we focus on learning, not blame — turning reflection into better systems next time.

## What You Can Expect From Me as a Leader

- Regular 1:1s with transparent communication and clear priorities.
- Context-rich decision-making and open rationale for strategic choices.
- A bias toward action balanced with thoughtful reflection.
- A psychologically safe environment where candor and curiosity are valued.

## What I Expect From My Team

- Ownership and initiative — see problems, not just tickets.
- Commitment to growth — continuous learning and intellectual honesty.
- Collaboration built on trust, humility, and respect.
- High standards and accountability — for yourself and the mission we share.

## How I Handle Challenges

### Conflict Resolution

I believe conflict, when handled correctly, is a source of growth. My approach starts with curiosity and empathy — understanding underlying motivations before jumping to solutions. I make space for each voice, focus on shared goals, and reframe discussions from “who’s right” to “what’s right.” When needed, I facilitate structured dialogues to get to resolution quickly while preserving relationships and trust.

### Crisis Management

In high-pressure situations, I focus on clarity, calm, and communication. I gather facts, prioritize immediate actions, and ensure everyone understands roles and next steps. I apply the “prioritize and execute” principle from Extreme Ownership: stabilize, communicate, learn, and then recover stronger. My goal is to lead with composure and transparency so the team feels supported and confident even under pressure.

### Difficult Conversations

I take a direct yet compassionate approach inspired by Radical Candor: care personally, challenge directly. I prepare by grounding the discussion in observable facts and focusing on shared intent — growth, performance, or alignment. During the conversation, I listen actively, avoid defensiveness, and ensure we walk away with clarity, accountability, and next steps.
